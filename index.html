12yo Language Arts — Weekly Rhythm

She has a strong creative voice already — this teaches mechanics around it, not instead of it.

  • Mon & Wed — Copywork from her current book + Khan Academy Grammar (free, start Unit 8: Syntax)
  • Tue — Big Juicy Conversation about her book, or a Paragraph Structure exercise (alternate weekly)
  • Thu — Freewrite, 5–10 min timed, no editing
  • Fri — Dictation + "why is that punctuation there?" + Poetry Teatime
  • Weekly — "Translate the Poem": rewrite one of her own lines as a standard, fully-punctuated sentence
  • Monthly — Punctuation Hunt: add punctuation back into a stripped passage

New Hobby Taste — Skill Rotation (Monday Kickoff)

One per week, cycling through, then repeats. Each gets ~10–15 min on its Kickoff morning.

1. Mental Math & Estimation

  • 5yo"About how many?" — guess a small pile of objects before counting together
  • 8yoRound prices/numbers to the nearest 10, then check the exact answer
  • 10yoEstimate the shopping cart total before checkout, compare to the receipt
  • 12yoQuick percentage/discount estimates before working the exact answer

2. Memory (Memory Palace)

  • 5yoChain game — "I went to the market and bought..." each person adds and repeats the list
  • 8yoA 3–4 stop mini palace using their bedroom — memorize a short list
  • 10yoA full 10-stop palace (their house) — memorize something in fixed order, like the planets
  • 12yoUse the palace for real content — the sequence of Revolutionary War events

3. Ask an Expert

All ages: before/after learning something new, write or say 2–3 genuinely curious questions you'd ask a real expert. Chase a real answer down sometimes — that's what makes it stick.

4. Time Estimation

All ages: guess how long a task will take, then time it and compare. Works best woven into normal moments (chores, errands, reading) rather than scheduled separately.

5. Touch Typing

  • 10yo & 12yoTyping.com (free, self-guided)
  • 8yoHome row letter games (beginner track)
  • 5yoSimple keyboard familiarity only — too young for touch typing proper

6. Map Reading

  • 5yoPoint to directions on a simple compass rose picture
  • 8yoFollow a hand-drawn treasure map around the house or yard
  • 10yoRead a real map — find locations, estimate distance using the scale
  • 12yoBasic orienteering — real compass, plot and follow a short route

Free map reading activity pack (compass rose, scale, key)

7. Body Language / Reading a Room

Core game, all ages: Feelings Charades — draw an emotion, act it out with face and body only, everyone else guesses.

  • 5yoJust the game, plus naming emotions on real faces during the day
  • 8yoAdd "what do you think they felt and how could you tell?" after a story or show
  • 10yoMismatch rounds — act one emotion, show a different one on your face, guess which is real
  • 12yoDiscuss context clues beyond the face — posture, tone, what's not being said

One-on-One Ritual Ideas

Same instinct as Poetry Teatime — consistent day/time, zero academic agenda, theirs alone.

Monday Kickoff Ritual

1. Dance party (5 min) — put on a song, everyone dances, no rules. Shakes off the weekend transition before anything else starts.

2. Weekly preview — read the week's theme out loud together (check the This Week tab). Build excitement: "this week we get to..."

3. Goal-setting — each kid names one personal goal for the week, academic or personal, their choice. Say it out loud or write it down.

4. Skill Spotlight (15–20 min) — a rotating self-improvement/enrichment segment, one category per week, cycling through all four:

  • Life Skill — a practical kitchen/household skill, age-scaled (measuring ingredients, following a recipe, a repair task)
  • Money & Enterprise — coin ID for the 5yo, a savings jar for the 8yo, needs-vs-wants budgeting for the 10yo, a mini business-idea brainstorm for the 12yo
  • Character & Mindset — gratitude practice, a growth-mindset discussion, a kindness challenge for the week
  • New Hobby Taste — its own 7-week sub-rotation, one skill per week, then repeats:
    1. Mental Math & Estimation
    2. Memory (Memory Palace technique)
    3. Ask an Expert
    4. Time Estimation
    5. Touch Typing
    6. Map Reading
    7. Body Language / Reading a Room

Full age-scaled practice for each of the 7 skills is in the Individual Work tab.

5. One thing to look forward to — each kid names one thing about the week ahead they're genuinely excited about.

No academic content on Mondays anymore — this replaces it entirely.

12yo Poetry Teatime

Tea, something nice to eat, poems read aloud together — hers or published poets'. No agenda. Anchors the whole language arts week.

5yo Tea Party or Storytime Snuggle

Small cups, a "fancy" snack, stuffed animals invited as guests, a picture book read together — or a simple baking morning (built-in counting/measuring, zero pressure).

8yo Comic Club or Snack & Sketch

Hot cocoa + comics/graphic novels, discussed like poems — favorite panel, what happens next. Or drawing invented gadgets/creatures together while snacking, no "right way" to draw.

10yo Snack & Strategy or Tinker Time

A standing one-on-one game night (chess, cards, strategy games) with a snack that's just his — or an old gadget to take apart and see how it works.

For the days you can't teach

Light Day Mode

Doctor appointment, sick day, just need a break — hand each kid this menu and let them pick. No supervision required, nothing to grade.

12yo Pick One

  • Free reading + an optional 10-min freewrite after, zero pressureWhatever book she's into right now
  • Music or dance free timeNo lesson, no assignment — her choice entirely
  • A self-directed creative projectPoem, sketch, playlist — if she wants a prompt: "design an album cover for a song that doesn't exist yet"

8yo Pick One

  • Comic-makingFold paper into a mini booklet, draw and write his own comic, no rules
  • LEGO or building challengeOne-line prompt: "tallest tower that holds a book" or "a creature with 6 legs"
  • Audiobook + drawingA story on audio (Libby works well) while he draws alongside it

5yo Pick One

  • Playdough or a sensory binDry rice/beans with cups and spoons, or playdough with cookie cutters — low mess, high entertainment
  • "Read" the picturesHand her a picture book she knows well, let her tell the story from the pictures
  • Sticker or coloring pageSimplest option, for the easiest possible day

All Three Together — Backyard Scavenger Hunt

Write a list of 5 things to find (a rock, something red, something soft, something round, something that smells good). Each kid works it at their own level. No supervision needed once the list is written.
